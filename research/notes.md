# Source notes — Recoverly

- **Source:** IdeaBrowser free Idea of the Day, October 19, 2026 — [public report](https://www.ideabrowser.com/hub/ideas/today)
- **Title:** Invoice auditor that catches supplier overcharges for restaurant groups
- **Category / index:** SaaS; IB Index 7.0
- **Revenue potential:** report estimates $8M–$15M ARR; year-one illustration $75K–$150K, while its detailed conservative napkin model yields about $47K in year one and $160K in year two. These are illustrative, not forecasts.
- **Execution profile:** moderate difficulty; $10K–$30K capital; 90–150 days to first revenue. Demands: sales 10/10, domain depth 9/10, trust 8/10, build 5/10, capital 4/10.
- **Buyer:** CFO/COO of a 5–100 location restaurant group, ideally beginning with a 15-location operator using a major food distributor.
- **Painful status quo:** AP teams receive frequent, long invoices and manually compare price changes with contracts, if they compare them at all. Existing AP products surface variances in a dashboard, leaving staff to assemble evidence, contact suppliers, follow up, and reconcile credits.
- **Narrow wedge:** one supplier, a 90-day lookback, and the full claim lifecycle: detect, document, prepare, follow up, and reconcile. The prototype stops at a human-approved claim packet.
- **Why now:** the report connects production-grade invoice automation with thin restaurant margins. It cites median pre-tax income of 2.8% for full service and 4.0% for limited service, with volatile food input pricing creating more opportunities for billing errors.
- **Evidence:** a Buyers Edge analysis reportedly covered 11,000+ invoices across 400 restaurants and found at least one overcharge on 35%, with typical overcharges around 1% of invoice dollars. A separate Restobiz-cited study reportedly found 22.7% of invoices had a line billed above the agreed price. PRGX describes an established AP recovery-audit category. These sources establish a pattern, not demand for Recoverly.
- **Competitors:** Craftable is the closest SKU/contract matching incumbent. MarginEdge ($350/location/month), xtraCHEF ($179–$279/location/month), Restaurant365 ($249–$749/location/month), and Ottimate cover adjacent restaurant AP workflows. PRGX covers enterprise recovery audits; Dining Alliance/Buyers Edge has purchasing leverage and data.
- **Demand signals:** public restaurant-owner discussions complain about unauthorized charges, distributor billing, and labor-intensive price comparison. Search volume for proposed solution terms was reported as zero, making founder-led outbound and warm introductions the likely starting channel.
- **Offer ladder:** free 90-day lookback for one distributor; single-vendor recovery at 25% of recovered value; all-vendor 12-month recovery agreement at the same contingency; ongoing forward monitoring.
- **Economics from the report:** 800 target groups; 300 worked accounts; assumed 4% lookback acceptance and 33% paid close; $600K annual distributor spend per unit; approximately 0.35% recoverable; roughly $7.8K contingency revenue per 15-unit group annually. Acceptance and close rates are explicitly assumptions.
- **GTM:** warm intros to multi-unit CFOs, then high-touch outbound with a one-page dollar-denominated recovery report. SEO and paid search are weak starting channels.
- **Biggest risks:** incumbents can add claim chasing; supplier retaliation/friction can hurt operators; a founder without restaurant-finance credibility faces a long trust ramp; contract terms and credits can be ambiguous; false positives damage trust.
- **AI/model strategy:** deterministic arithmetic and contract evidence are authoritative. OCR/LLMs may map messy line items and draft correspondence later, but must expose confidence and source lineage and never autonomously submit claims in the early product.
- **Cited domains in the report:** fsrmagazine.com, restobiz.ca, prgx.com, researchandmarkets.com, sec.gov, makershub.com, duck-hub.com, tariffproofkitchen.com, Reddit communities. Links and claims should be independently verified before commercial use.
